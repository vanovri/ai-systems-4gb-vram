Grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Citation Enforcement

Production RAG systems must prevent hallucinations by enforcing strict provenance and explicit abstention rules.

1. Inline Citation Provenance
Every factual claim in a grounded RAG response must carry an inline citation tag in the format [source_file#chunk_id]. Inline citations make answers auditable by letting users and automated evaluators verify that every sentence maps directly back to a specific document page and chunk excerpt.

2. Anti-Hallucination Refusal Policy
When a user asks an out-of-scope question whose concepts do not appear in the indexed corpus, the retrieval stage yields low cosine similarity, weak BM25 scores, and poor content-term overlap. Rather than fabricating an answer from parametric guesses, the QA engine must check calibrated relevance thresholds and emit an explicit anti-hallucination refusal stating that the indexed documents do not contain sufficient supporting evidence.

3. Chunking Strategy and Overlap
Recursive character and sentence chunking splits documents along natural boundaries (paragraphs, newlines, sentence punctuation) up to a configurable chunk_size. Including a modest chunk_overlap (for example 15 to 20 percent) prevents facts that straddle chunk boundaries from being severed during retrieval.
